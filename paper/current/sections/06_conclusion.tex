\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we presented a sequential, four-tranche data engineering and graph construction framework that transforms unstructured narrative disclosures and heterogeneous financial tables from SEC Form 10-K filings into a longitudinal \textit{Hierarchical Network of Networks} for 108 corporations tracked by the Invesco QQQ Trust (2021--2025). By coupling a three-stage XBRL/HTML extraction fallback pipeline with schema-enforced LLM serialization, our methodology resolves long-standing parsing bottlenecks in longitudinal SEC filing analysis. The resulting two-scale graph formulation $\Hnet$ captures both intra-firm operational architecture---linking operating segments, products/services, competitors, risks, and geographic revenue streams---and configurable inter-firm macro-topologies ordered by market capitalization.

This methodological foundation opens several immediate avenues for empirical complex network analysis. In ongoing work, we are utilizing this dataset to: (1) characterize longitudinal topological evolution, degree distributions, and community modularity across different macro-projection lenses $\Omega \in \{\text{Competition}, \text{Risk}, \text{Geography}\}$ from 2021 to 2025; (2) quantify cross-scale coupling between a firm's internal micro-complexity (e.g., segment concentration and risk diversity) and its macroscopic centrality; and (3) simulate multi-scale shock propagation across shared supply, risk, and geographic revenue channels within the Nasdaq-100 ecosystem.
