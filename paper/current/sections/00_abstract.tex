\begin{abstract}
Traditional financial network models treat corporations as static, atomic nodes, obscuring the complex internal architectures of multi-segment enterprises. In this paper, we present a comprehensive data engineering and graph formulation framework that transforms unstructured narrative text and heterogeneous financial tables from SEC Form 10-K filings into a longitudinal \textit{Hierarchical Network of Networks} ($\Hnet$). Focusing on a continuous cohort of 108 firms from the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) index between 2021 and 2025, our sequential four-tranche pipeline couples a multi-stage XBRL/HTML fallback extractor with schema-enforced Large Language Model (LLM) serialization. The resulting dataset is grounded in a rigorous 10-table relational schema that maps over 8,600 atomic products, 7,900 competitive threats, and 19,400 specific risk factors directly to their corresponding operating segments or enterprise roots.

Our methodology explicitly solves the network ``hairball'' problem by enforcing structural bifurcation, preventing localized operational risks from polluting macroscopic enterprise topology. Empirical validation demonstrates exceptional corpus fidelity: the pipeline achieves a 99.91\% LLM grounding rate to native text blocks, while an accounting invariant safeguard successfully maps \$42.4 trillion in cumulative operational revenue to narrative segment nodes. Furthermore, longitudinal analysis reveals that 76.9\% of corporate operating segments underwent structural rewiring within the 5-year window, empirically validating the necessity of modeling intra-firm temporal evolution. By bridging internal micro-complexity with macroscopic inter-firm topology, this framework provides a robust computational dataset for multi-scale financial network science.
\end{abstract}
