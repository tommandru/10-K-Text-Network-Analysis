\begin{abstract}
Financial networks typically model corporations as single-layer, featureless nodes connected by market return correlations, board interlocks, or supply chains, thereby obscuring the internal operational structures that drive macroscopic firm interactions. In this paper, we introduce a sequential, four-tranche data extraction and graph formulation framework that constructs a longitudinal \textit{Hierarchical Network of Networks} from annual SEC Form 10-K filings for a static cohort of 108 U.S. corporations tracked by the Invesco QQQ Trust (Nasdaq-100 Index) from 2021 to 2025. Inspired by waterfall structures in structured finance, our pipeline gates processing into four dependency-driven stages: (1) cohort verification from quarterly Form N-PORT filings ($N=108$ continuous filers), (2) hybrid parsing of narrative disclosures (Items 1 and 1A) paired with a three-stage fallback pipeline (XBRL, keyword parsing, and native HTML anchor splitting) across 2,160 financial statement instances (Items 7 and 8), (3) Large Language Model (LLM) serialization into a nested JSON schema, and (4) instantiation of a two-scale graph. At the micro-level, each firm is modeled as a rooted sub-network branching into Products/Services, Competitors, Risk Factors, and Geographic/Segment Revenue attributes. At the macro-level, the 108 firms are ordered by market capitalization and connected via configurable inter-firm projections across competitive, risk, and geographic dimensions.
\keywords{Financial Networks, Hierarchical Network of Networks, SEC Form 10-K, Large Language Models, Nasdaq-100, Multilayer Graphs}
\end{abstract}
