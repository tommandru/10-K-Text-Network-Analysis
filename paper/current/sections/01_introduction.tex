\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern equity markets function as tightly coupled complex systems in which competitive pressures, operational bottlenecks, and macroeconomic shocks propagate across corporate boundaries. Network science has provided foundational tools for characterizing these interdependencies, predominantly representing publicly traded firms as atomic nodes linked by stock return correlations~\cite{mantegna1999}, credit exposures~\cite{battiston2012}, ownership ties~\cite{vitali2011}, or text-based product similarities~\cite{hoberg2016}. However, collapsing a diversified enterprise into a single featureless node masks the internal micro-architecture that governs its external connectivity. A corporation's macroscopic vulnerability to systemic risk or competitive overlap is fundamentally structured by its internal portfolio of operating segments, specific product and service lines, geographic revenue concentrations, and multidimensional risk factors. Capturing both scales simultaneously requires moving beyond single-layer graphs toward a \textit{Hierarchical Network of Networks} formalism~\cite{gao2012,kivela2014} that explicitly couples intra-firm operational sub-graphs with inter-firm market topology.

Constructing such a multi-scale representation empirically requires longitudinal, audited firm-level disclosures. In the United States, annual Form 10-K filings mandated by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) offer the most comprehensive record of corporate operations. Specifically, Item~1 (\textit{Business}) and Item~1A (\textit{Risk Factors}) detail product ecosystems, competitors, and operational risks in unstructured narrative text, while Item~7 (\textit{Management's Discussion and Analysis}) and Item~8 (\textit{Financial Statements}) report quantitative revenue decompositions by operating segment and geographic region. Yet, transforming multi-year Form 10-K archives into aligned network structures at scale presents severe data engineering challenges: corporate filers employ heterogeneous HTML formatting, customize eXtensible Business Reporting Language (XBRL) tags across reporting periods, and interweave segment-specific narratives with enterprise-wide disclosures.

In this work, we present an end-to-end, sequential ``tranche-based'' data extraction and network construction framework that transforms raw SEC filings into a longitudinal Hierarchical Network of Networks for leading U.S. firms tracked by the Invesco QQQ Trust across the 2021--2025 window. Adapting the sequential waterfall paradigm from structured finance, our architecture processes data in dependency-driven tranches where each validation gate must clear before downstream graph instantiation proceeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:tranche_pipeline}). Our primary contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sequential Waterfall Extraction Pipeline (Tranches 1--2):} We isolate a longitudinal panel of 108 U.S. corporations from 20 quarterly Form N-PORT filings and implement a hybrid parsing and three-stage fallback methodology across 2,160 XBRL statement instances, keyword parsers, and native HTML string-splitting rules to achieve complete longitudinal coverage across Items 1, 1A, 7, and 8.
    \item \textbf{LLM-Driven Nested JSON Serialization (Tranche 3):} We design a schema-enforced Large Language Model (LLM) extraction workflow that reconciles narrative disclosures with segmented financial tables, mapping each firm-year filing into a hierarchical JSON tree spanning products/services, segment vs.\ general competitors, operational vs.\ external risks, and segment/geographic revenues.
    \item \textbf{Two-Scale Hierarchical Network Formulation (Tranche 4):} We formalize a longitudinal two-layer graph model $\Hnet$ pairing intra-firm micro-networks $\Gmicro$ (weighted by segment and geographic revenue attributes) with a configurable macro-level topology $\Gmacro$ across the 108 market-cap-ordered corporate nodes.
\end{enumerate}
