\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The structure of the modern global economy is fundamentally interconnected, with financial shocks, supply chain disruptions, and competitive pressures propagating rapidly across corporate boundaries. Consequently, network science has become a critical lens for modeling systemic risk, market structures, and financial contagion. However, the vast majority of financial network models treat corporations as static, atomic nodes. This atomic assumption fails to capture the reality of modern conglomerates and multinational enterprises, which are complex organizations composed of diverse operating segments, distinct product lines, and fragmented geographic footprints.

Regulatory disclosures, particularly the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Form 10-K, contain rich, granular data detailing this internal corporate complexity. Management's narrative discussion of business operations (Item 1) and risk factors (Item 1A), combined with audited quantitative segment financials (Items 7 and 8), offer a blueprint of the firm's internal architecture and external market exposure. Yet, parsing this data into a structured mathematical graph remains notoriously difficult. Filings are highly unstructured, varying wildly in taxonomy, formatting, and structural continuity across different firms and fiscal years.

In this paper, we overcome these extraction bottlenecks by introducing a sequential, schema-constrained pipeline to construct a longitudinal \textit{Hierarchical Network of Networks} ($\Hnet$). We extract data from a static cohort of 108 Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) firms continuously filing between 2021 and 2025. Rather than treating a firm as a single vertex, our model unpacks the corporate node into a hierarchical micro-network ($\Gmicro$) of category hubs, operating segments, and atomic leaf nodes. These micro-networks interlock to form a dynamic macroscopic topology ($\Gmacro$) spanning temporal, competitive, and semantic axes.

Our contributions are threefold: (1) A robust 10-table relational database schema that bridges unstructured textual narratives with audited ASC 280 financial data; (2) A bifurcated routing architecture that empirically prevents network ``hairballs'' by disentangling segment-specific threats from enterprise-wide boilerplate; and (3) A rigorous empirical validation demonstrating 99.91\% model grounding fidelity and the structural alignment of \$42.4 trillion in operational revenue.
